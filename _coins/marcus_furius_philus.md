The coin celebrates the victory of [Gnaeus Domitius Ahenobarbus](https://romanrepublic.ac.uk/person/1630/?facet_view=person_search&praenomen=Gnaeus&nomen=Domitius&re_number=&f=&n=&tribe=&cognomen=Ahenobarbus&other_names=&era_from=&era_to=&q=) and [Quintus Fabius Maximus Allobrogicus](https://romanrepublic.ac.uk/person/1594/?facet_view=person_search&praenomen=&nomen=&re_number=&f=&n=&tribe=&cognomen=&other_names=Allobrogicus&era_from=&era_to=&q=) over the Allobroges and Arverni in southern Gaul 121 and 120 BCE. Both were awarded triumphs for these feats and Fabius was given the agnomen Allobrogicus. The reverse has a clear military nature and shows Roman victrix (victorious) crowning a military trophy surrounded by shield, armor, and gallic war horns (carynx). On the obverse, Yarrow sees the two-faced god Janus as representing both generals. 

While these decisive actions were occurring in Gaul, Fabius' consular colleague [Lucius Opimius](/coins/l_opimius/) was fomenting political violence in Rome. His murder of Gaius Gracchus, under the cover of a *senatus consultum ultimum*, established the norm for the mob violence and disorder that characterized political life in the Republic during its final century.
