## Prologue: 113–105 BC

**113 BC**
- P. Licinius Nerva strikes [RRC 292/1](/coins/p_licinius_nerva/) (113/112), the voting-bridge reverse. Crawford identifies the moneyer as the praetor of 104.[^nerva]
- L. Cassius Longinus Ravilla presides over the special court trying three Vestal Virgins.[^vestals] The Cassii later recall it on [RRC 413/1](/coins/cassius_longinus_voting/) (63) and [RRC 428/2](/coins/q_cassius_longinus/) (55). Family background, not the war itself.

**109/108 BC**
- Mn. Aquillius, the future consul of 101 who will end the war, strikes RRC 303/1 (Sol/Luna).[^aquillius303] *(Not in the collection.)*

**105 BC**
- Arausio: the Cimbri destroy the armies of Q. Servilius Caepio and Cn. Mallius Maximus; Caepio's command is revoked. (verify)

## The war years: 104–100 BC

**104 BC**
- Marius begins his run of consulships (104–100). (verify)
- Nicomedes of Bithynia says his men were enslaved by the publicani; the Senate orders allies unlawfully enslaved to be freed. (verify)[^diodorus-outbreak]
- Nerva, praetor of Sicily, starts freeing slaves, then stops under pressure from the slaveholders. The slaves he turns away revolt, and the Second Servile War begins (Salvius, who takes the name "Tryphon" as king, and Athenion). (verify)[^diodorus-outbreak]
- Lex Cassia of the tribune L. Cassius Longinus: anyone condemned by the people or stripped of command loses his seat in the Senate. Crawford ties [RRC 428/3](/coins/q_cassius_longinus_eagle/) (55 BC) to this law.[^lexcassia] Usually said to be aimed at Caepio. (verify)
- L. Saturninus strikes [RRC 317](/coins/saturnius/) (Crawford's date: 104). Crawford identifies him as the tribune of 103 and 100, and the Saturn quadriga as a pun on his cognomen.[^saturninus]
  - Die counts:[^saturninus-dies] 317/1: 100 obverse dies; 317/2: 10 (the rare one); 317/3a: 370 obverse / 462 reverse. No separate count for 3b.
- c. 104: Saturninus, quaestor at Ostia in charge of the grain supply, is removed during a grain shortage and replaced by Scaurus. Year disputed. (verify)[^ostia]

**103 BC**
- Saturninus' first tribunate: land for Marius' African veterans; the treason law (*lex de maiestate*). (verify)
- L. Licinius Lucullus is sent to Sicily against Tryphon; he wins a battle but fails to take Triocala and is later prosecuted. (verify)[^lucullus]
- C. Norbanus prosecutes Caepio over Arausio (103 or later). (verify)

**102 BC**
- C. Servilius takes the Sicilian command and fails as well. Athenion leads after Tryphon dies. (verify)
- Marius destroys the Teutones at Aquae Sextiae. (verify)

**101 BC**
- Consul Mn. Aquillius takes Sicily and is said to kill Athenion himself.[^aquillius401] (verify the single combat)[^athenion]
- Marius and Catulus destroy the Cimbri at Vercellae. (verify)

**100 BC**
- The war ends; Aquillius finishes the mop-up in 100/99. (verify)
- Saturninus' second tribunate: colonies for veterans; a land law with an oath every senator must swear. Q. Caecilius Metellus Numidicus refuses the oath and goes into exile. (verify)[^numidicus]
- Grain: the Lex Appuleia frumentaria (103 or 100; disputed). (verify)
- Piso & Caepio strike [RRC 330/1](/coins/capio_piso/), AD FRV EMV EX S C, struck by a quaestor at Ostia and an urban quaestor for the grain supply.[^pisocaepio]
  - Crawford discusses the occasion of the issue on p. 73. Read it before tying the coin to Saturninus' law.
  - Possible twist: a quaestor Caepio used force against Saturninus' grain law.[^adherennium] Is he the coin's Caepio? Was he the son of the Arausio Caepio? (verify both)
- December: Saturninus killed in the Senate house after the *senatus consultum ultimum*. (verify)

## Aftermath and later echoes on coins: 99–55 BC

**99/98 BC**
- Numidicus recalled from exile; his son earns the cognomen "Pius" for campaigning for the recall.[^pius]
- Aquillius tried for extortion; M. Antonius tears open his tunic to show his scars, and he is acquitted. (verify)[^deorat]

**88 BC**
- Aquillius executed by Mithridates with molten gold poured down his throat. (verify)[^mithr]

**81 BC**
- Metellus Pius, a Sullan commander, strikes [RRC 374/2](/coins/metellus_pius/). The obverse type alludes to the cognomen won for his father's recall.[^pius] It answers Saturninus' oath of 100.

**71 BC**
- Mn. Aquillius Mn.f. Mn.n. strikes [RRC 401/1](/coins/mn_aquillius/): Virtus; a warrior raising a fallen figure, SICIL. Crawford: it alludes to his grandfather's *beneficia* to Sicily.[^aquillius401] The coin that looks back on the war most directly.
  - Coin page says 61* BC; Crawford says 71. Reconcile.

**63 BC**
- L. Cassius Longinus strikes [RRC 413/1](/coins/cassius_longinus_voting/): a voter casting a V (*uti rogas*) tablet. Crawford ties it to the 113 Vestal law.[^vestals] A Cassian echo, not the war.

**55 BC**
- Q. Cassius strikes [RRC 428/2](/coins/q_cassius_longinus/) and [RRC 428/3](/coins/q_cassius_longinus_eagle/): 428/2 for the Vestal trials and the ballot law, 428/3 for the Lex Cassia of 104.[^lexcassia] The eagle stands for *imperium*, not monarchy.[^lexcassia]

## Structure idea

- First wave, struck during the crisis: Nerva (113), Aquillius (109/8), Saturninus (104), Piso & Caepio (100).
- Second wave, struck by descendants as family memory: Metellus Pius (81), Aquillius (71), Cassius (55).
